# How the November 27 EVS frames were validated

The 30-second KCBS-2 capture contains 1,500 audio slots. Weather-record checksums support 355 EVS frames. Codec and programme measurements support an additional 795 frames. The remaining 350 slots carry explicit erasures. The frame ledger identifies each slot by counter, phase, four-frame cycle, and position within that cycle. [Frame ledger](../examples/nov27/frames.csv) · [Capture-specific masks](../dumps/nov27-measured-masks.json)

## From radio packets to a checksum-supported frame

A complete 1.2-second counter group has three data units. Each contains five cycles of four 20 ms audio frames. The receiver takes the transmitted mode byte and 32 payload bytes from their recorded locations. The receiver applies the mask at eleven zero-based payload offsets, 0, 3, 6, …, 30. In the accepted fourth-position masks the final octet is zero, leaving that payload byte as received. Within a group the same eleven-byte mask applies to a given position in all its cycles. Changing group or position requires a separately supported mask. Applying masks at these offsets preserves the received frame order. [Receiver implementation](../scripts/render_measured_evs.py)

Each data unit also carries 16 bytes of scrolling text. Repeated Korean bulletin phrases, sequence headers and the `BB` fill supplied candidate plaintext. A complete record let the receiver check the CRC-16 transmitted after its header and body. For example, the ordinary record at counter 43854908, phase 1 decodes to a header and body of 94 bytes whose calculated CRC is `C73D`. A distinct weather-title record at 43854901, phase 1 gives `032B`. Nine ordinary records and one title record support 16 mask bytes per counter from 43854887 through 43854910. The first eleven unmask position 1. The remaining five can seed position 2. The 23 complete counters and two final phases supply 345 + 10 checksum-supported audio frames. ## How a candidate became an inferred frame

The earlier Pyongyang FM broadcast and a Wangjaesan CD contain the same performance. Previously established receiver frames aligned their clocks. Opposite-half calibration and, in a separate test, calibration excluding the target counter and its neighbors reduced reuse of the observations being scored. The two reference recordings supply related acoustic measurements. Every released EVS word comes from the radio packets.

For positions 3 and 4, linear-prediction coefficients, TCX pitch and spectral envelopes, or HQ band energies ranked candidates for the first four masked offsets. For the next offset, payload byte 12, the search considered all 256 values where the native EVS header permitted them. It carried eight leading branches into a bounded search of the remaining bytes. A candidate had to satisfy the codec's arithmetic bit budget on the received frames. For mixed-mode groups, a further check re-encoded eligible HQ quantizer words and compared the resulting bits with the received words.

One example is position 3 at counter 43854891, where all 15 frames use transform-coded excitation (TCX). The sign reader varied the final masked byte through all 256 values and kept only physical sign-bit addresses pointing to the same transform coefficient every time. At those addresses the FM and CD predictions matched **257 and 256 of 259** clear sign bits. Moving the clocks by 740 ms gave **127 and 123**. Both recordings matched 93 of 95 terminal signs. Each selected the same eleven-byte mask on the full group, the odd and even cycles, and the first seven and last eight cycles. Every terminal bit had at least two observations on every split. [Per-source counts and mask](../examples/nov27/validation/evidence.json)

This TCX-only rule accepted seven of 15 eligible group/position pairs, or 105 frames. On seven first-position controls with the expected result withheld, it recovered four full masks exactly and abstained on three. The first four bytes were supplied for one of the four exact controls. The standard EVS decoder reproduced all 2,377 sign addresses used for the seven selected masks. A separate sign-first search revisited six unresolved positions. Across its 48 branches and 246 finalists, none had a terminal byte legal on all 15 frames. These searches establish uniqueness within their tested candidates. ## What changed in the audio

On 785 comparable newly supplied slots, the final radio waveform scored **0.930 against FM and 0.926 against CD** in signed, normalized 75–3,500 Hz comparisons. Swapping added words between slots of the same mode and frame position gave 0.035 and 0.034. Corrupting payload bits gave 0.045 on both. A 740 ms shift gave −0.016 on both. At the last counter, 43854910, two final-byte values for position 4 had valid syntax. The pooled and odd-cycle waveform rankings disagreed. Its ten position-4 slots remain erased.
