# The 96-byte trailer repeats across receivers and changes across groups

The 3205 kHz member recording contained 1,102 complete, distinct trailers. There were **1,081 pairs of consecutive counters** for which the complete trailer was available at both ends. The 96-byte value and each of its three fragments changed in all 1,081 pairs. Between 92 and 96 individual byte positions changed in each pair. Of 103,776 byte comparisons, 395 kept the same value. Gaps in reception were excluded from the cadence test. [Whole-recording counts](fingerprint.json)

An EVS word spans 20 ms. Each 400 ms data unit carries 20 EVS words plus one trailer fragment. Three successive data units form a 1.2-second group carrying 60 words and one complete 96-byte trailer. With zero-based, half-open offsets in a 716-byte data unit, the fragments are phase 0 `[668,698)`, phase 1 `[665,698)` and phase 2 `[664,697)`. The phase-zero counter occupies `[664,668)`. The phase-two byte at 697 was zero in every checked packet. The assigned packet observations establish the group cadence. They do not locate the instant at which the transmitter generated or updated each fragment. [Earlier layout audit](../../../../scripts/audit_capture_layout.py) · [Per-phase checks](results.json)

## Independent receiver comparison

At matching counters the 3205 and 6140 kHz receivers agreed on **90/90 complete trailers**. A trailer requires the last CRC-valid packet of each of the three phases. Requiring all 12 packets and all three data-unit CRCs at both stations gave **81/81** matches. Both stations saw both ends of 69 consecutive counter pairs. All 69 complete values changed. Thirty individual byte positions across those pairs retained their value. Shifting the second receiver by one counter gave **0/80** complete matches. These comparisons use the previously published 119-counter overlap. [Original packet receipts](../README.md) · [Matched, missing and shifted results](results.json)

We also tested whether the trailer can align two recordings whose coverage overlaps. The 3,335 complete data units in the longer 3205 kHz recording supplied 2,219 distinct 33-byte phase-one or phase-two fragments. We searched their combined index with each of 187 qualifying 6140 kHz packets, trying both possible 33-byte windows without passing the peer's counter or phase to the lookup. It returned **183 unique correct coordinates, zero wrong or ambiguous matches, and four misses**. All four missing coordinates were absent from the index. The peer sample came from an already identified overlap. A future recording could repeat some bytes or have no counterpart in the index. [Lookup code](fingerprint.py) · [All denominators](fingerprint.json)

We then applied the lookup to **previously unassigned** native receiver rows. A unique 33-byte trailer match proposed a counter and phase. At least one additional packet in the same row had to pass CRC and independently name that same coordinate by its entire 179-byte body. Of 17 eligible rows at 3205 kHz, three met both conditions. At 6140 kHz, 48 of 59 did. Three further 6140 kHz rows had a unique fragment match and lacked a second witness. The 51 placements added 174 packet coordinates to the individual receiver records, corresponding to 54 and 758 additional slots with all transmitted word octets present, respectively. Every packet was already present at the other station. Across the combined recordings the complete-group count stayed at 1,106 and the complete wire-slot count at 67,019. The additional packet supplies a cross-check and could itself be used to look up the row. ## Member report

A member separately reports 2,996 distinct group trailers, occasional matches among 26 proposed checksum layouts, and zero complete matches between the trailer changes and the known text-mask changes in 195 held-out pairs. They also report agreement on mutually received packets for two groups after changing decoding iterations and receive-window starts. We have the screenshot. The underlying trial definitions and the referenced line-108 log were unavailable for this comparison. The reported sample and our archive may overlap. These observations leave the purpose of the variable bytes open. The successful recording-alignment test uses their identity at a counter and supplies no clear EVS payload bits.

In a follow-up the member reports 2,994 consecutive pairs, an average of 95.6 changed octets out of 96, and changes in the known mask prefix in all 1,422 adjacent pairs with text CRC support. Their held-out comparisons found no four-octet counter with the tested fixed offset or XOR and reported correlations of magnitude at most 0.066 at the tested lags. We do not have their line-114 log or raw observations. Each count describes its own selection. If corresponding octets at consecutive counters were independent uniform bytes, the expected number that differ would be `96 × 255 / 256 = 95.625`. The observed average by itself has little power to distinguish formats.

Our 3205 kHz archive provides a separate control. In **37 consecutive group pairs**, the same EVS mode octet persisted for all 120 slots. The trailer changed in **37/37**. At counters **43908424 and 43908425**, every mode octet was `F8` and all 96 trailer octets changed. This rules out a deterministic function of the group's 60 mode octets alone in this recording. Mode equality leaves the encoded audio free to vary. Run `python mode_control.py` here to reproduce the counts. [Test definition](mode-control.md) · [Per-condition counts and witnesses](mode-control.json)

The DRM control channels identify the service as a packet-data application. Its standard conditional-access flags and type-2 announcements can be checked directly in the signalling recordings. AES Key Wrap also illustrates how little a byte count identifies. If the concatenated trailer were exactly one full RFC 3394 output, a length of 96 would correspond to 88 input octets. With the RFC 5649 padded variant it could accommodate 81 through 88 input octets. Neither length calculation supplies a format identifier or a test of the actual received bytes.
